From the shoebox

A complaint, filed

The Blackwater Inn, August 1986

Dear Mrs Voss,

I write, as I have written every August for fifteen years, to put three matters before you.

First, breakfast. Eight o'clock is not early. The pike rise at six, and by eight they have risen, eaten and gone home.

Second, boat number three. It leaks. I have told your boatman. Your boatman has told me it doesn't. One of us is wet to the knee every morning, and it is not your boatman.

Third, the fish. Forty-one pounds, taken on Tuesday off the north shallows: the largest pike ever landed on this lake, as your own scales will tell you. I offered it to the bar, mounted at my own expense. I understand you have declined.

I shall, of course, be back next year.

Yours, as ever,
B. Quayle (Room 138)

In the margin, in Eleanor's hand: “Filed.”

About the author

J.M. Ashcombe

J.M. Ashcombe has been reading detective novels since childhood, beginning with a borrowed Agatha Christie and never really stopping. The appeal has always been the same: a closed circle of suspects, a fair set of clues, and the moment the last piece falls into place.

That interest grew into a long habit of following real cold cases, the ones reopened decades later because someone finally looked again at an old register, a letter or a photograph. Ashcombe's books borrow that idea. Every case begins with a record, a victim and a list of names, and the reader does the detective work.

The Blackwater Ledger is Book 1 in the Find the Killer series. Each title is a complete case with one fair answer, and new cases are on the way.
